What Quantum Park is

INDOOR MANUFACTURING AND WELL-PAYING JOBS, BUILT FOR MARSHALL COUNTY

Quantum Park is a proposed 124.96-acre industrial subdivision on Highway 99, on land beside Volunteer Materials' existing quarry. The plan brings indoor, light manufacturing — and the steady paychecks that come with it — while keeping surrounding homes and farmland protected by wide, permanent buffers.

The conceptual plan divides the property into four developable outparcels, wraps the eastern edge and the highway frontage in landscaped open space, sets aside frontage land for a vocational center, and prohibits mining across the entire site.

The three commitments that anchor the plan

What we're putting in writing

Commitment 01

No mining. NO DATA CENTERS.

Mining and data centers are prohibited across the entire subdivision. The site is being rezoned and built for indoor manufacturing, with 25–30 year commercial leases written so that mining stays prohibited for decades to come.

Commitment 02

Permanent buffers

Roughly 14 acres of open space — owned and maintained by the Property Owners' Association — run as a continuous 150-foot landscaped buffer along the eastern boundary and the Highway 99 frontage.

Commitment 03

A vocational center

About 10 acres along the highway frontage are reserved for a vocational training center — an answer to a workforce-training gap people in the county have pointed to for years.

Keeping trucks off Highway 99

LESS TRUCK TRAFFIC ON THE PUBLIC ROAD

Part of the site will include an on-site concrete block plant served by the neighboring Volunteer Materials quarry. A dedicated cut-through routes those quarry trucks off Highway 99 — pulling that traffic off the public road and reducing the truck movement neighbors see today.

The 60-foot internal right-of-way keeps movement inside the park, with fewer driveways meeting the highway.

Answers to the questions we hear most

FAQS

01
Will there be mining?
No. Mining is prohibited across the whole subdivision, and the long-term leases are structured to keep it that way.
02
Is this a data center?
No. This is indoor manufacturing under roof, with low water use and a footprint that stays inside the property line.
03
Do the buffers go away over time?
No. The 150-foot buffers along the eastern edge and the highway are dedicated open space held by the POA, meant to last as long as the park does.
04
What about truck traffic?
A dedicated cut-through will route quarry trucks serving the on-site concrete block plant off Highway 99 — pulling that traffic off the public road and reducing the truck movement neighbors see from the adjacent quarry.
05
How much water does it use?
We're deliberately recruiting businesses with low water use, and steering away from high-demand, water-intensive operations. Indoor light manufacturing of the type planned here uses relatively little water, stays well within regulatory limits, and protects local waterways. The goal is a park that adds jobs and tax base without asking the county's water system to stretch further than it already does.
